# Licensing — the three layers

Not legal advice; a working map so nothing has to be routed around later. Check each licence yourself
before you ship, and keep every asset's `prov.json`.

## 1. The code

RPG_Forge is MIT. Use it for anything.

## 2. The models (the weights you download)

A model licence governs whether you may run the model at all and for what. The registry
(`models/models.json`) records each model's licence and a `commercial_ok` flag; the rule the forge
follows: **assets that ship in a commercial game come only from `commercial_ok` models**; the others
are for experiments. As of November 2026:

| Model | Licence | Shipped assets? |
|---|---|---|
| SDXL 1.0 and most SDXL fine-tunes | CreativeML Open RAIL++-M (use-based restrictions, commercial use allowed) | yes |
| FLUX.2 [klein] 4B (and 4B Base) | Apache 2.0 | yes |
| FLUX.2 [klein] 9B, FLUX.2 [dev], FLUX.1 [dev] | FLUX Non-Commercial License | no (buy a licence or don't ship) |
| Qwen-Image, Qwen-Image-Edit | Apache 2.0 | yes |
| LoRAs from Civitai/Hugging Face | each its own — read the page | per LoRA; record it in prov.json |

Fine-tunes inherit their base model's licence *plus* whatever the fine-tuner adds; a pixel-art SDXL
LoRA on Civitai may forbid commercial use even though SDXL allows it. The `prov.json` of every render
names the checkpoint so the audit is a grep.

## 3. The images (what comes out)

A permissive model licence puts no claim on outputs. The larger question is copyright in the output
itself. In the United States the Copyright Office's January 2025 report holds that works entirely
generated by AI are not copyrightable — prompting alone, however iterative, does not make the prompter
the author — while a human's selection, arrangement and modification of AI output can be protected, and
AI-generated material inside a larger human work (a game) does not stop the game being protected.
Practically:

- Raw generated tiles and cards are, in the US view, effectively public domain — anyone could reuse them.
  The game as a whole, its code, writing and music composition remain yours.
- Hand-finishing (pixel clean-up, palette work, composition into scenes) puts human authorship into the
  assets that matter most. Finish the heroes by hand; let the forge do volume.
- Keep the provenance so you can say exactly which assets are which.

## 4. Storefront disclosure

Steam requires developers to disclose player-facing AI-generated content (art, audio, text) in the
content survey; the disclosure appears on the store page. Development tools that leave nothing in the
shipped game (coding assistants) do not need disclosing. The forge's `prov.json` files are that inventory:
list the asset kinds, say the humans reviewed and finished them, done.

## 5. Palettes and data

Palettes on Lospec are shared by their authors for use in art; credit the author (`palettes/README.md`).
Public-domain geodata for the planned overworld job — Natural Earth, US Census TIGER/Line, USGS GNIS,
USGS 3DEP, NLCD — carries no licence strings; OpenStreetMap (ODbL, share-alike on derived data) is
deliberately not used.
