**G2 · Review sheets a model can read** *(CLI — finish tonight).* *Why:* the bake-off grids are 5–10 MB PNGs with a hundred cells each; Claude reads them one staged image at a time and cannot see detail at that scale. The hand-made sheets in `out/bakeoff/sdxl_v1/review/` were the workaround. *What:* `python -m forge review <suite|batch> --compact`: sheets of at most **12 cells**, longest side ≤ 2,000 px, JPEG ≤ 1.5 MB, every cell labelled in the picture (checkpoint · LoRA · the tool fields that differ — not the ones that are the same), one sheet per prompt per page, plus `review_index.json` (sheet → the job ids in reading order, so a verdict can be turned into `rate` calls without guessing) and a **pairs** mode (`--pairs A,B`: two cells side by side at full height, with the 1:1 detail crop under each) for the finishing bake-off, where the difference is in the detail. `review_compact.bat`. *Done when:* `finish_v1` and `style_v1` (once they have renders — until then the existing `sdxl_v1`) each produce sheets under the size limits and an index, with tests for the paging and the label logic.

**G3 · Big scenes — painting past one frame** *(plug-in job type `scene_big`; proven on the GPU).* *Why:* above. A scrolling street cannot be painted in one pass, and refining a 6144 × 3072 image in one pass will not fit in 12 GB. *What:* the standard two-stage method, as ops: (1) the whole scene painted small from the whole greybox, for composition and one consistent light; (2) upscaled to the target; (3) **tiled refine** — `forge/ops/tiles.py`: `plan(size, tile, overlap)` → the tile rectangles (tile ≤ 1344 × 768 or whatever the job says, overlap ≥ 128 px, edges snapped so no sliver tiles), `split`, and `merge` with a feathered blend across each overlap, deterministic; each tile is an ordinary `repaint` of its crop at low denoise with `control` set to **tile** (and canny from the greybox crop when the job has a greybox) so the content cannot wander, all tiles sharing prompt, seed and sampler; (4) a **seam score** per overlap (mean colour and gradient difference between the two tiles' versions of the shared strip) written into the contact sheet, worst seams outlined. A `scene_big` plug-in (`forge/plugins/scene_big.py`) that runs the stages itself, calling the existing paint and upscale code as a library — it does not edit it. `scene_kit` accepting `big: true` is a live-file edit: **`WIRE_NIGHT_3.md`.** *Done when:* `python -m forge op tiles-demo` splits a 6144 × 3072 test image, runs a fake "repaint" (any deterministic filter) on each tile and merges it back with no visible seams and a score sheet; unit tests for plan (coverage, overlap, no slivers), merge (a flat image stays flat; a gradient stays a gradient) and the score; **and on the GPU:** one existing 1920 × 1080 painting from `out/` taken to 3840 × 2160 through `scene_big` (four to six tiles) with its seam sheet — small on purpose.

**G4 · Inpaint, and with it: states of a room, and filled back planes** *(plug-in job types `scene_state` and `inpaint`; proven on the GPU).* *Why:* `bills_house` needs `peace_b` and `peace_c` — the same room six and ten years on (the rack grows, the boy's room grows up). Two fresh paintings would be two different rooms. And `scene_layers fill: "inpaint"` has been a stub since Night 2. *What:* (a) an SDXL **inpaint graph** builder (pure dict; read the installed ComfyUI's node list for the right nodes — do not write it from memory; the plain `VAEEncodeForInpaint`/`SetLatentNoiseMask` route with the job's own checkpoint needs no extra model, so no new licence); (b) `forge/ops/state_mask.py`: two greybox renders of the same map (base and state — the game writes them) → a mask of what changed, grown and feathered, with the changed area's share of the frame reported, and a refusal with a plain message if more than ~35 % changed ("this is a new painting, not a state"); (c) a `scene_state` handler: base painting + mask + a prompt for only the changed region → the state painting, everything outside the mask **bit-identical** to the base (composited back after the sampler — test this), then the kit's companions regenerated only where the mask is; (d) `scene_layers`' `fill: "inpaint"` routed to the same graph — a live-file edit, so **`WIRE_NIGHT_3.md`**. *Done when:* graph and mask are unit-tested, the bit-identical guarantee has a test, `op state-mask` works on two greyboxes from `out/_tests/`, **and one `scene_state` job has run on the GPU** on any existing painting with a hand-made rectangular mask: the contact sheet shows base · mask · result · difference, and the difference is zero outside the mask.

**G5 · `kit check` — is this kit safe to approve?** *(CLI — finish tonight.)* *Why:* a kit can look right and be wrong: a companion at the wrong size, a floor the depth model read as a wall, a spawn point standing on a painted table.
